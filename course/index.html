Computer Vision, from Pixels to 3-D

A hands-on course that starts with a pixel and ends with a 3-D reconstruction of a street. Every method is first written from scratch in NumPy, then compared with the OpenCV function you would use in practice — and every figure on these pages is the real output of the code.

About this course#

This course grew out of two university courses I took and the projects I built around them: Robotic Vision in the mechatronics graduate program at UFRN (Brazil) and Image Processing (TIM) at INSA Rouen Normandie (France), plus the computer vision I use in my research on autonomous drones and boats. I rewrote all of it as one path, in English, cleaned up the code, fixed the mistakes I made the first time, and added the explanations I wish I had had.

Each lesson follows the same rhythm:

  1. The idea — what problem we are solving and the maths behind it, kept as short as possible.
  2. From scratch — an implementation in plain Python/NumPy that you can read line by line.
  3. The OpenCV way — the one-liner you would use in a real project, and a check that both agree.
  4. Run it — every lesson is a single script. Run it, change a parameter, run it again.
  5. Pitfalls — the bugs I actually hit, so you don't have to.

Who it is for#

Students and engineers who know some Python and want to understand computer vision instead of copying function calls. You need high-school maths; a bit of linear algebra (matrices, vectors) helps from Part I on and becomes essential in Part V. No deep learning is required: this is the classical toolbox that every vision pipeline — including the neural ones — still relies on for calibration, geometry, pre- and post-processing.

How to follow it#

Start with lesson 00 to install the environment and download the code and data (one zip, about 20 MB). Parts I to IV build on each other; Part V (geometry and 3-D) needs lessons 11–14; the two case studies in Part VI reuse a bit of everything.

Where this leads

Lessons 15–17 are the foundations of two of my projects: the boat perception pipeline (stereo depth + GPS georeferencing) and drone localisation in a pre-built map with ORB-SLAM3.

Part I Foundations

00 Setup and how the course code works Install Python, OpenCV and friends in a virtual environment, download the code and data, and learn the two-flag convention every lesson script follows. venv · pip · OpenCV · project layout 01 Images are arrays A digital image is a NumPy matrix. Learn how OpenCV stores it, why data types cause half of all image-processing bugs, and how masks replace loops. NumPy · dtype · BGR · channels · ROI · masks 02 Sampling, quantization and interpolation A digital image is a continuous scene sampled on a grid and quantized to a few levels. See what happens when we use too few samples or too few levels, and how to resize an image properly. quantization · histograms · k-means · aliasing · nearest / bilinear 03 Geometric transforms Rotate, scale, shear and change the perspective of an image with matrices. Homogeneous coordinates turn all of them into one matrix product, and inverse mapping turns that into an image without holes. rotation · homogeneous coordinates · affine · homography · warping

Part II Enhancement

04 Histograms, point operations and LUTs Change the brightness, contrast and dynamic range of an image by remapping its gray levels. Equalise histograms, transfer the "look" of one photo to another, and do all of it with look-up tables. thresholding · contrast stretch · equalisation · CLAHE · gamma · histogram matching 05 Noise and spatial filtering Identify the kind of noise in an image, remove it with mean, median and Gaussian filters, understand convolution as the one operation behind most of them, and clean up a badly lit barcode. Gaussian & salt-and-pepper noise · PSNR · convolution · median · filter2D · background removal

Part III Edges, shapes and colour

06 Edge detection Edges are where the intensity changes fast. Find them with first derivatives (Sobel, Prewitt) or second derivatives (Laplacian, difference of Gaussians), and turn the result into a clean edge map. gradient · Sobel · Prewitt · Laplacian · DoG · zero crossings 07 The Canny edge detector Build the most widely used edge detector step by step — smoothing, gradient, non-maximum suppression, double threshold and hysteresis — and check it against cv2.Canny. Gaussian blur · non-maximum suppression · double threshold · hysteresis 08 The Hough transform Turn edge pixels into lines by letting every pixel vote for all the lines that could pass through it. Detect road lanes, and circles too. parameter space · accumulator · peak detection · HoughLinesP · HoughCircles 09 Mathematical morphology Erode, dilate, open and close binary images with a structuring element. Clean a noisy shape, make a damaged QR code readable again, and find patterns with hit-or-miss. structuring elements · erosion · dilation · opening · closing · gradient · hit-or-miss · connected components 10 Colour spaces and colour segmentation RGB is how screens work, not how to reason about colour. Convert to CMYK, YUV and HSV by hand, then use hue to recolour objects and to segment and locate a toy car. CMYK · YUV · HSV · inRange · hue shift · moments · centroid

Part IV Features

11 Corners and tracking Corners are points you can find again in another image. Detect them with Harris and Shi-Tomasi from the structure tensor, refine them to sub-pixel accuracy, and follow them between video frames with template matching and Lucas-Kanade. structure tensor · Harris · Shi-Tomasi · NMS · cornerSubPix · NCC · optical flow 12 Keypoints and descriptors — FAST, ORB and SIFT Detect keypoints with FAST, give them an orientation, describe them with binary BRIEF strings and match them with the Hamming distance — then see why ORB steers its descriptors and how SIFT and the ratio test compare. FAST · intensity centroid · BRIEF · Hamming distance · ORB · SIFT · FLANN · ratio test 13 Homography and RANSAC Estimate the homography between two views of a plane from point matches with the DLT and the SVD, and make it robust to wrong matches with RANSAC — written from scratch, then with cv2.findHomography. DLT · Hartley normalisation · SVD · RANSAC · inliers · warping

Part V Geometry and 3-D

14 Camera model and calibration Write down how a 3-D point becomes a pixel — extrinsics, perspective, lens distortion, intrinsics — then recover the camera's parameters from chessboard photos, undistort images and place a virtual cube on the board. pinhole model · intrinsics K · distortion · calibrateCamera · reprojection error · solvePnP 15 Stereo vision Two calibrated cameras see depth. Calibrate and rectify a stereo pair, compute disparity maps with block matching and SGBM, convert them to metres and point clouds, and georeference an object seen from a boat. epipolar geometry · stereoCalibrate · rectification · StereoBM / SGBM · depth = fB/d · point cloud · GPS 16 Structure from Motion Recover the camera's motion and a 3-D map of the scene from a single moving camera — essential matrix, cheirality, triangulation and PnP — on 15 frames of the KITTI driving dataset. essential matrix · recoverPose · triangulation · solvePnPRansac · scale ambiguity · visual odometry 17 Visual SLAM with ORB-SLAM3 Everything from lessons 11–16 assembled into a real-time system. Understand ORB-SLAM3's three threads, run it in Docker with ROS 2 on a recorded bag, and use a saved map to localise a drone. tracking · local mapping · loop closing · bag of words · Docker · ROS 2

Part VI Case studies

18 Case study: lungs and vessels in CT scans Medical images are not photos. Read CT slices in Hounsfield units, choose display windows, segment the lungs with thresholds, connected components and contours, segment the vessels inside them, and screen 21 patients. NIfTI · Hounsfield units · windowing · connected components · contours · masks · areas in mm² 19 Case study: fingerprint identification Five fingerprints were found at a crime scene, damaged in five different ways. Thin the ridges, detect minutiae with the crossing number and hit-or-miss, clean them up, restore the crime-scene prints and match them against a database of 20 people. thinning · crossing number · hit-or-miss · convex hull · pre-processing · alignment-based matching
